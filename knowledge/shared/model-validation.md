# Sample Size & Model Validation

A model can look great on 10 picks and still be noise. Before you scale your stakes, build a sample of evidence that the edge is real — not luck.

## Why Sample Size Matters
- 10 picks tells you almost nothing; variance dominates
- ~50–100 settled picks are needed for a meaningful hit-rate signal
- Tracking results reveals process leaks (biases, FOMO, bad leg selection)
- Scaling stakes before the edge is proven is gambling, not analytics

## How to Build Your Sample in VibeMeGood
1. Add picks to the Entry Builder as normal
2. Use a small, consistent stake while you gather data
3. Log every entry and let it settle (WIN/LOSS/PARTIAL)
4. Review performance in the Journal and Review Dashboard

## When to Scale Up Stakes
Increase size only when ALL of these are true:
- 50+ settled picks logged
- Entry hit rate ≥ 55% over the sample
- No single leg dominates all winners (diversified edges)
- A bankroll plan is in place (Kelly fraction, max units per entry)

## How the Shark Helps
The assistant analyzes, constructs, and evaluates entries, and sizes stakes
with the Kelly Criterion based on your stated edge. It will push back when you
force action on a thin sample or skip the workflow — small, validated steps
beat big, unproven swings.

## Conservative Signals on Thin Data
Early on, many features show conservative signals because data is thin.
This is correct behavior:
- 50 PLAY props = not enough game logs yet
- LOW SAMPLE badges = correct
- Charts / presets locked = not enough logged history
As you log results nightly, these gates open automatically. The model improves
with every settled entry. Do not shortcut the gates — they protect you from
acting on unvalidated signals.
