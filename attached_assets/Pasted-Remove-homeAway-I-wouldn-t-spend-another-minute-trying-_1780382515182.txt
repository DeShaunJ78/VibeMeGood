What I would do next

1. Investigate the Blocked Shots anomaly FIRST

Not because it’s the biggest source of error.

Because it’s the least understood.

A 90.5% MaxCalError should immediately trigger curiosity.

Questions:

* How many samples?
* Which bucket?
* Which line?
* Which players?
* Is it a ZIP issue?
* Is it a data issue?
* Is it one weird season?

If it turns out to be:

8 samples

then who cares.

If it turns out to be:

2,000 samples

then that’s a major discovery.

Before changing NBA assists or turnovers, I’d find out which it is.

⸻

2. NBA Turnovers and Assists

These are the next obvious candidates.

Why?

Because they’re exactly the kind of stat that tends to violate Normal assumptions.

Especially turnovers.

A player can:

* have 1
* have 2
* have 3
* suddenly have 8

That’s classic overdispersion.

Negative Binomial is a natural fit.

⸻

3. Rebounds

I’d put rebounds behind turnovers and assists.

Rebounds tend to be more stable.

Not perfect.

But less obviously broken.

⸻

4. Ignore combination stats for now

I would not touch:

* PRA
* Points + Rebounds
* Assists + Rebounds
* Hits + Runs + RBIs

Yet.

Those are second-order problems.

You’re talking about sums of correlated variables.

The complexity jumps dramatically.

The payoff may not.

⸻

The surprising winner

Honestly?

The biggest lesson from this whole exercise may be:

Routing accuracy mattered more than model complexity.

You didn’t build a transformer.

You didn’t build an ensemble.

You didn’t add 50 factors.

You simply stopped forcing the wrong distributions onto the wrong stats.

And the model got dramatically better.

That’s a great sign because it means the improvements are grounded in probability theory rather than curve fitting.
