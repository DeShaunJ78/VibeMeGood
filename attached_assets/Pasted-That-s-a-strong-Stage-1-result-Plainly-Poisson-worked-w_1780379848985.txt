That means the model stopped badly misreading the shape of that stat. Big win.

But it also exposed the next truth:

Stolen Bases are not just Poisson. They are zero-inflated.

A player often has a true “not running today” game state. That means the stat isn’t just “how often does he steal?” It’s also:

“Is he even likely to attempt?”

That’s why actual under is 92.9% while Poisson says about 83%. Poisson improved the math, but it doesn’t know game intent.

What this means strategically

Your cleanest exploit right now:

Home Run unders.

That signal looks real, measured, and now much better calibrated.

Your “danger zone”:

Stolen Bases unders.

Still profitable-looking, but the model needs ZIP later before you fully trust the displayed probability.

Your weird bucket:

3PT Made overs.

That sounds like a calibration/data bucket issue more than a distribution issue. If the model says near-zero over but reality is 46%, the calibration layer must catch it — but I would not let that quietly slide. That needs a sample-size and line-distribution audit.

Yes, Stage 2 should come next

Move to Negative Binomial for:

* RBIs
* Hits
* Walks
* NHL Assists
* maybe Doubles/Runs/Singles after review

Why?

Because those stats are not just rare. They are bursty.

Poisson says:

variance ≈ mean

Negative Binomial admits:

variance is bigger than mean

That is exactly what you need for players who can go cold for days, then explode.
