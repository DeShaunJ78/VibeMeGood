The stolen bases finding is valuable

The biggest lesson isn’t ZIP.

It’s this:

The mean is wrong.

You discovered something deeper than a distribution issue.

The model may be overestimating the baseline steal rate of the population PrizePicks chooses to post.

That’s a market-selection problem.

PrizePicks isn’t posting:

* Ronald Acuña
* Elly De La Cruz

everywhere.

They’re posting lots of players whose true steal expectation is tiny.

That means:

Future opportunity

Build stat-family priors using:

* PrizePicks-posted players
* not league-wide players

That could be a larger improvement than ZIP itself.

My read on Stage 4

I’m much more cautious here.

The first three stages fixed obvious distribution mismatches:

* Normal → Poisson
* Normal → NegBin
* Normal → ZIP

Those are slam dunks.

Stage 4 is trickier.

Candidate stats

* Pass Yards
* Rush Yards
* Receiving Yards

These aren’t sparse counts.

They’re continuous-ish.

They have:

* skew
* floors
* ceilings
* matchup dependence

A log-normal may help.

A large-r NegBin may help.

But the gains may be much smaller than Stages 1–3.
